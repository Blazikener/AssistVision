AssistVision glasses

A clip-on camera for ordinary glasses that photographs a meal when it is served and when the plate is cleared. The laptop works out how much was eaten.
What you build
  • 1 camera board, 1 battery, 2 printed clips
  • 4 solder joints, no breadboard
  • About 2 hours, about ₹2,000–2,900
How it is used
  1. Meal served: press the button, turn your head slowly across the plate (4 s).
  2. Plate cleared: do the same.
  3. The laptop compares the two and logs % eaten.

Renders show the printed parts from hardware/glasses/cad/out on a generic frame. The device has not yet been built and tested; record your own measurements.

Battery safety. Only a single-cell 3.7 V LiPo. Never reverse the wires: red to BAT+, black to BAT−. Do not puncture, bend or charge it unattended.

Check the clip first. Push the printed clip onto the temple arm. It should grip firmly. Too loose: add a strip of foam tape inside the hook. Too tight or too thin: measure your temple arm and re-generate the parts: 
python hardware/glasses/cad/glasses_mount.py   # edit temple_t / temple_h at the top first

3Flash the firmware (before soldering)

  1. Install Arduino IDE 2, then Boards Manager → “esp32” by Espressif.
  2. Click the camera module onto the board’s B2B connector (it clicks), and plug the board into the laptop with USB-C.
  3. Open hardware/firmware/assistvision_glasses/assistvision_glasses.ino.
  4. Copy secrets.example.h to secrets.h and type your Wi-Fi name and password (2.4 GHz only). Never commit secrets.h.
  5. Tools → Board → XIAO_ESP32S3, Tools → PSRAM → OPI PSRAM, then Upload.
  6. Open the Serial Monitor at 115200. You should see WiFi up IP 192.168.x.x and one long LED flash.
python src/glasses_client.py --host assistvision-glasses.local status

If .local names do not resolve on your laptop, use the IP from the Serial Monitor instead.

Test a capture now: meal --id test --phase served saves 13 photos to data/meals/test/served. Open them: the image should be upright and sharp. If it is upside down, send POST /camera?vflip=1.

4Solder (4 joints)

  1. Battery: on the back of the XIAO, red wire to the BAT+ pad, black wire to BAT−. Check with a multimeter before connecting the cell.
  2. Button (optional): one leg to D1, the other to GND. Skip it to use the BOOT button through the lid window.
  3. Slide heat-shrink over each joint.
Polarity matters. A reversed LiPo can destroy the board instantly.

5Assemble

  1. Slide the board into the camera pod, camera first, so the lens sits in the square window.
  2. Lead the battery wires out of the bottom slot.
  3. Press the orange lid in. The USB-C port and the BOOT/charge-LED window stay open. Add a drop of glue if loose.
  4. Push the battery into its pod.
  5. Clip both pods onto the right temple arm: camera pod at the hinge, battery behind it.
  6. Tape the wire along the arm.

Charge by plugging USB-C into the board (it charges the LiPo itself). The small red LED by the USB port shows charging.

6Use it at a meal

  1. Laptop and glasses on the same Wi-Fi. Run, before the meal arrives: 
    python src/glasses_client.py --host assistvision-glasses.local meal --id 2026-11-02-lunch-r1 --phase served --wait-button
  2. Sit where the diner sits, look at the plate from about half a metre, press the button.
  3. Turn your head slowly from left to right for about 4 seconds while the LED blinks (13 photos).
  4. Two blinks = all photos taken. Six fast blinks = some photos failed; press again.
  5. After the meal, run the same command with --phase cleared and repeat from the same seat.
Keep the two printed ArUco squares next to the plate in both captures: they give the laptop a fixed reference.
