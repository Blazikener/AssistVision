\textbf{Nutrition5k has no volume ground truth.} The dataset provides
overhead RealSense depth and weighed mass, not food volume. We therefore score
monocular models against the volume integrated from sensor depth over the same
dish region (food plus plate rim above the support plane). This measures how
well a model reproduces a depth sensor, not how well it measures food. Our
attempt to separate food from plate with a second plate-floor plane fit gave a
food volume that barely tracks weighed mass (Spearman $\rho=0.25$,
Pearson $r=-0.02$, $n=503$), so we do not use it as a target. Absolute food-volume
accuracy is reported only on synthetic scenes with exact mesh volume.

\textbf{Synthetic-to-real gap.} Blender scenes have perfect camera intrinsics,
controlled lighting and procedural textures. Real OV2640 frames add rolling
shutter, auto-exposure drift, lens distortion and JPEG blocking on low-texture
food. The resolution and JPEG ablations bound part of this gap but not all of it. Textureless food already fails in simulation (Sec.~\ref{sec:synth}); an active texture projector or image-space segmentation would be needed for it.

\textbf{No physical measurements yet.} The glasses have not been printed,
flashed or worn. Fit on a real temple arm, image sharpness during the head
sweep, battery life, heat and Wi-Fi transfer time are untested, and the first
turntable rig's bench tests have not been run either. None of the numbers in
this paper come from hardware. Timings are CPU timings on 8-vCPU cloud VMs.

\textbf{One seat, one side.} A sweep from the carer's position sees the near
side and top of the food, never the far side, so each volume is biased; the
share is reliable only to the extent that the bias is the same in the served
and the cleared scan (E6). A hand, cup or spoon in front of the plate, a
missed button press or a plate moved between the two scans breaks the pair.
The ArUco squares must lie beside the plate for metric scale; the share itself
needs only a consistent scale between the two scans.

\textbf{A share, not grams.} In the earlier turntable design a load cell
would have weighed the plate; the glasses cannot. They tell the carer that, say,
70\,\% of the serving went. To get grams, the kitchen would weigh each portion
once when it is plated, and we would multiply. That only holds if what is left
is as dense as what was served, which fails for a thali where the rice has gone
and the dal is left.

\textbf{No clinical or user study.} The app, the alert rule and the weekly
report have not been used by older adults, families or clinicians. Usability
for people with poor eyesight or tremor, acceptance, alert fatigue and any
effect on intake or outcomes are untested, and the residents in the app's demo
data are fictional. A pilot in a ward or care home, with weighed plate waste as
reference and staff and family feedback, is the next step.
