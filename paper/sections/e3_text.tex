Table~\ref{tab:synth} gives the results. With the pre-registered footprint
rule, dense MVS has a median absolute error of 4.6\,\% [2.0, 6.1] on the 16
textured-food scenes, and 13 of 16 are within 10\,\%. Steel plates (3.0\,\%,
8 of 8 within 10\,\%) do better than ceramic (5.9\,\%, 5 of 8). Banana leaf
gives $-1.9$ and $+7.8$\,\%. The textureless white ceramic gives plate-surface
noise of 1.9--7.4\,mm, against 0.9--1.2\,mm on the textured steel. The three
textured-food misses are all on ceramic: the curry heap ($-26.0$\,\%, below),
the 38\,mL small portion ($-30.1$\,\%; 4.5\,mm plate noise is a large share of
an 18\,mm-high portion) and the tall mound ($+16.8$\,\%).

\textbf{Textureless food fails.} With the food texture removed, dense MVS gives
$-1.4$\,\% (curry), $-20.8$\,\% (idli), $-69.4$\,\% (rice + dal) and
$-82.9$\,\% (rice mound), a median of 45.1\,\%. Without texture on the food,
the matcher has nothing to triangulate on its surface. Real rice and dal are
textured, but smooth sauces and plain white foods are not, so this is the
main limit of a passive-camera rig.

\textbf{Failure case.} \texttt{curry\_heap\_\_ceramic} is $-26.0$\,\%: its
6.2\,mm plate noise raises the threshold above the low outer skirt of the
heap, which is then cut from the footprint. The failure is mostly but not
always repeated. Five runs of the identical input on this machine gave
$-26.0$, $-26.0$, $-24.8$, $-23.1$ and $+0.2$\,\%, and one run on a second VM
gave $+0.1$\,\%. OpenMVS is not deterministic on the textureless plate, and the
noise estimate, and with it the threshold, moves between runs. The post-hoc hysteresis rule
recovers it ($-1.0$\,\%) and lowers the textured-food median error to
2.2\,\%, but its bias is $+5.5$\,\% and it overestimates the tall mound on
ceramic by 46.1\,\%, lumpy rice by 24.2\,\% and the thali by 17.9\,\%, where
plate noise connects to the food. No single height
threshold works on every scene. We keep the pre-registered rule as primary
and conclude that \emph{separating food from plate}, not depth accuracy, is
now the dominant error of the multi-view pipeline. Image-space food
segmentation projected onto the height field is the natural next step.
\textbf{Sparse is not enough.} Sparse SfM points alone have a median error of
91.3\,\% [79.5, 99.5] over all 22 scenes, nearly always an underestimate:
feature points concentrate on the textured mat and food edges, leaving most of
the food surface empty. Two steel scenes happen to land within 10\,\%
(thali $-0.8$\,\%, tall mound $+3.4$\,\%), but the steel median is still
78.9\,\%.
Densification is therefore required, and it is the main cost of a scan.
Dense reconstruction takes about 119\,s per scene, against 18\,s for sparse on the VM CPU (VM evidence).
