\textbf{Protocol.} When the meal is served, the carer wearing the glasses
presses the button and turns their head slowly across the plate; when the
plate is cleared they do the same again (Fig.~\ref{fig:care};
\texttt{glasses\_client.py meal --phase served|cleared}). Each 13-frame burst
is reconstructed on the host into a food volume, $V_s$ at serving and $V_l$ at
clearing, and the share eaten is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intake}
f = 1-\frac{\min\!\big(\max(V_l,0),\,V_s\big)}{V_s}.
\end{equation}
A served volume under 5\,ml is treated as an empty plate. A leftover more than
10\,\% larger than the serving (food added, a different plate, a failed scan)
is stored with a flag and kept out of the alert rule instead of being clipped
silently (\texttt{intake.photo\_intake}).

\textbf{Why a share, not grams.} The glasses carry no scale. Grams from images
would need a density for every food, and on real mixed dishes even
sensor-measured volume tracks weighed mass poorly (E2). A share needs neither:
the plate, the food and its density are the same in both scans, so they cancel
in the ratio. This is the quantity nurses already record by eye in quarters of
a plate~\cite{heighington2022visual}, measured instead of guessed. Where the
kitchen weighs each portion, grams eaten follow as $f\,m_s$, assuming the
leftover has the same density as the serving.

\textbf{Alerts and reports.} A meal is \emph{low} if $f<\tau$; an alert is
raised when the last $k$ scored meals are all low, and only once per run. The
defaults $\tau=50\,\%$ and $k=3$ are prototype values, not clinical cut-offs;
the clinician sets both per person. The weekly report lists, per day, the
number of meals, the eaten share and the count of low meals. It does not
report energy or nutrients.

\textbf{App.} The companion app (Flask and SQLite, Fig.~\ref{fig:app}) has one
page per older adult with the alert banner, weekly figures, two-week chart,
recent meals and the circle of care (family members and doctor). Meals arrive
through \texttt{/api/meals} or are entered by hand. The endpoint takes either
served and left grams (the earlier weighing rig) or the two reconstructed
volumes from the glasses, which it turns into a share with the rule above and
tags as a glasses meal. Running the reconstruction and posting the result is
still a manual step on the host. Images stay on the host. In this
prototype alerts are recorded in the app but not sent by SMS or e-mail. Unit
tests cover the intake arithmetic, the alert rule (including firing once per
run) and the client-to-app path.
