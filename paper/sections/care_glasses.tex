\textbf{Protocol.} When the meal is served, the carer wearing the glasses
presses the button and turns their head slowly across the plate; when the
plate is cleared they do the same again (Fig.~\ref{fig:care};
\texttt{glasses\_client.py meal --phase served|cleared}). Each 13-frame burst
is reconstructed on the host into a food volume, $V_s$ at serving and $V_l$ at
clearing, and the share eaten is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intake}
f = 1-\frac{\min\!\big(\max(V_l,0),\,V_s\big)}{V_s}.
\end{equation}
A served volume under 5\,ml is treated as an empty plate. A leftover more than
10\,\% larger than the serving (food added, a different plate, a failed scan)
is stored with a flag and kept out of the alert rule instead of being clipped
silently (\texttt{intake.photo\_intake}).

\textbf{Why a share, not grams.} There is no scale on the glasses. Grams from
pictures would need the density of every food, and E2 already showed that even
sensor-measured volume follows weighed mass badly on real mixed dishes. A share
avoids both problems. The same plate with the same food is in both scans, so
plate size and density drop out when one volume is divided by the other. Nurses
already write this number down when they mark a quarter or half a
plate~\cite{heighington2022visual}; here it is measured. If the kitchen does
weigh portions, grams eaten are $f\,m_s$, assuming what is left is as dense as
what was served.

\textbf{Alerts and reports.} We call a meal \emph{low} when $f<\tau$. When the
last $k$ scored meals are all low, the app raises one alert and then stays quiet
until the run breaks. We start with $\tau=50\,\%$ and $k=3$. These are our
defaults, not clinical cut-offs, and the doctor changes them for each person.
The weekly report has one line per day: meals, share eaten, and how many were
low. Energy is not estimated. Protein is, dish by dish, as described below.

\textbf{App.} The companion app is a small Flask site on SQLite
(Fig.~\ref{fig:app}). Each older adult has one page: the alert banner on top,
the week's figures, a two-week chart, the last few meals and the circle of care,
meaning the family members and the doctor who see that page. A meal comes in
through \texttt{/api/meals} or is typed in by a carer. The endpoint takes served
and left grams from the earlier weighing rig, or the two reconstructed volumes
from the glasses. For the glasses it works out the share with the rule above
and marks the meal as a glasses meal. Someone still has to run the
reconstruction on the laptop and post the result. The photos never leave the
laptop. Alerts are saved in the app; this prototype does not send them by SMS or
e-mail yet. Our tests check the arithmetic, that an alert fires once per run and
not again, and the path from the client to the app.

\textbf{Which dish was left, and its protein.} A share of the whole plate
hides the case that matters most on an Indian menu: the rice is finished and the
dal is left. The rice gave energy, but most of the protein was in the dal. The
host therefore also splits the plate into dishes. It takes the centre frame of
each burst, warps it to a top view with the two mat markers, and marks as food
every pixel that differs from the bare rim in colour or local texture. The
served food is clustered into the $K$ dishes on the menu by colour and position,
and each cleared food pixel joins the nearest served dish. A dish's eaten share
is $1-(A_\text{left}/A_\text{served})^{1.5}$, since a mound that shrinks evenly
keeps its volume proportional to its area to the power 1.5. The kitchen enters
the protein in one portion of each dish once, from its recipe or the Indian food
composition tables, and the app multiplies. The daily total is compared with
1.0\,g per kg of body weight, the low end of the 1.0--1.2\,g/kg that the PROT-AGE
group recommends for healthy older adults~\cite{bauer2013protage}; the doctor can
change it. After three short days in a row the family and the doctor get one
message. If a dish was left (under 75\,\% of it eaten on average) the message
names it, e.g. ``Most often left: Sambar''. If almost everything was eaten and
protein was still short, it says the menu is short instead. That is a job for
the kitchen, not a sign of poor appetite. The low-intake alert cannot
separate these cases: in the demo data one resident eats most of every meal, so
her low-intake alert never fires, yet she stays under her protein target and
the protein alert does. Dietitians have long used photos of plate waste to
estimate what was eaten~\cite{williamson2003photo}. What we change is who takes
the photo (the carer, without stopping) and who sees the result (the family and
the doctor, the same day).
