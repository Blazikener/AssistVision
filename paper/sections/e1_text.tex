\textbf{Anchoring on the table fails from overhead.} All three relative models
have a median volume error of 94.8--99.4\,\% under the deployable anchor
protocol (Table~\ref{tab:n5k}), even though their predicted table is flat to
0.6--0.7\,mm RMS: the affine fit reproduces the table and collapses the food
relief. The anchor fit is badly conditioned on most dishes
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cond}), as expected when the only reference is a plane almost
perpendicular to the optical axis. This is the quantitative reason the rig
views the plate at $45^\circ$ and uses the marker mat and multiple views.

\textbf{Depth metrics do not predict volume.} AbsRel is 0.016--0.045 for every
model and protocol, yet volume errors span 14--99\,\%. Food relief
(1--3\,cm) is a small fraction of the 36\,cm camera distance, so a model can
score well on depth and still lose most of the volume. We therefore rank
models on volume, not on AbsRel.

\textbf{Oracle upper bound.} With an oracle fit DA-V2-S is best: 37.4\,\%
[33.0, 41.9] median volume error with its own support plane and 14.1\,\% with
the sensor's plane. The paired Wilcoxon test with Holm correction separates it
from DA-V1-S ($p=5.2\times10^{-11}$) and MiDaS-DPT-Hybrid
($p=5.5\times10^{-53}$). Under the anchor protocol DA-V1-S and DA-V2-S are not
distinguishable ($p=0.31$). The gap between the two plane choices shows that
fitting the support plane in predicted depth causes more than half of the
error; in the rig the marker mat provides this plane independently of the
network.

\textbf{Coverage and failures.} 498 of the 503 usable dishes were evaluated
for every model. The remaining five failed in the plane fit (degenerate table
region) and are counted as failures, not dropped. Latency is 1.4--1.9\,s per
image on the VM CPU, measured while other jobs shared the machine (VM evidence,
an upper bound). The metric models (ZoeDepth, metric DA-V2, Depth Pro) are
implemented in the same benchmark but had not finished at the time of
writing, so the raw protocol is not yet reported.
