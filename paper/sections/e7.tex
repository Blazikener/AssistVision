E7 reuses the 12 E6 pairs. Three of the six plates hold more than one dish
(a thali of rice, dal and curry; rice with dal; two idli), each on steel and on
white ceramic, so there are 20 dishes, 14 of them sharing a plate. The truth for
each dish is $1-V_\text{left}/V_\text{served}$ from the scene meshes. Only the
centre frame of each burst is used; nothing is reconstructed. Estimated items
are paired with true dishes by position (Hungarian assignment). The colour
threshold (12 Lab units), the texture threshold (6 grey levels above the rim),
the 9-pixel vote window and the exponent 1.5 were fixed before the run. One
change came after a first run: a unit test on drawn plates showed that the
$7\times7$ texture window added a 3-pixel ring of bare plate around every dish
and that this ring was handed to the wrong dish. The mask is now eroded by 3
pixels, and Table~\ref{tab:e7} reports the run after that fix
(script \texttt{analyze\_items.py} in \texttt{experiments/synth}).

Without the exponent the median error is 24.3 points: the area a dish covers
shrinks more slowly than its volume, so area alone says too little was eaten.
The plate materials swap places compared with E6. White ceramic does well here
and steel does badly, because the weak step is now the food mask rather than the
3D match. On steel some reflections on the bare plate pass as food. On ceramic,
white rice is partly lost against the plate: the rice on the rice-and-dal plate
covers 165\,cm$^2$ in the steel mask and 19\,cm$^2$ in the ceramic one. The worst
dish is the dal next to that rice on steel, read as 10\,\% eaten when 62\,\% had
gone.

The alert needs something coarser than the share: which dish was left. On all
6 plates with more than one dish, the dish with the lowest estimated share was
also the one with the lowest true share. Four of the 6 dishes that were
finished were read as at least 90\,\% eaten, and 3 of the 4 untouched dishes as
at most 10\,\%. Six plates is a small sample and they are renders, so this shows
that the split works on clean images, not that it is reliable on a real thali.
